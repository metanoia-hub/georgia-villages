## History
- Makalatia documented Tbatana as a departure point for Tsova-Tush pilgrims to the Sameba shrine. ([source](https://mdevari.ge/articles/4225/))
- Shavkhelishvili describes Tbatana as part of the land granted to the Tush by Georgian kings, and records a local account of a Bartishvili quarter near the lake. ([source](https://mematiane.ge/product-details.php?id=9581))
- A 2016 Alvanelebi account reports that the seasonal cottages were destroyed during the Pankisi events and that Tsova-Tush families later began rebuilding. It dates a royal grant of Tbatana to 1798. ([source](https://tirine.wordpress.com/2016/06/29/%E1%83%A2%E1%83%91%E1%83%90%E1%83%97%E1%83%90%E1%83%9C%E1%83%90/))
- Gustav Radde visited in 1876 and recorded the settlement as Батани, used by Tsova-Tush pastoralists. He traces their migration to Indurta and Sagirta in the highlands and to Alvani in the lowlands. ([source](https://www.vostlit.info/Texts/Dokumenty/Kavkaz/XIX/1860-1880/Radde_G_I/text9.htm))

## Crafts And Traditions
- Makalatia records that Tsova-Tush pilgrims set out from Tbatana for the Sameba shrine in Tsovata during Atnigenoba. ([source](https://mdevari.ge/articles/4225/))
- Alvanelebi describes sheep passing through Tbatana to summer pasture, alongside cow’s-milk cheese, curd and clarified butter made at the village for use or sale in the lowlands. ([source](https://tirine.wordpress.com/2016/06/29/%E1%83%A2%E1%83%91%E1%83%90%E1%83%97%E1%83%90%E1%83%9C%E1%83%90/))
- Rustavi 2 covered the Tbatanoba festival at Tbatana in 2024. ([source](https://rustavi2.ge/ka/news/289912))
- Gustav Radde observed flocks and a seasonal encampment with white felt tents during his 1876 visit. ([source](https://www.vostlit.info/Texts/Dokumenty/Kavkaz/XIX/1860-1880/Radde_G_I/text9.htm))
