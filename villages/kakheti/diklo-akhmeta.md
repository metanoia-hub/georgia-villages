## History
- The 1917 Tiflis Governorate table records the settlement as Дикло and lists all 194 residents as Georgian. ([source](https://pop-stat.mashke.org/georgia-ethnic-loc1917-east.htm))
- Makalatia’s 1933 survey records the following local surnames in 1930–31. Bukuraidze, Beladishvili, Nebieridze, Imedidze. ([source](https://www.batsav.com/pages/tusheti-population-census-makalatia.html))
- The Protected Landscape administration records that a Dagestani incursion destroyed Old Diklo and that residents relocated to another slope around the beginning of the 19th century. ([source](https://www.tushetipl.ge/en/article/11))
- The 2014 protected-areas management plan dates forced Soviet resettlement from Tusheti to the lowlands to the late 1950s. This describes the regional policy. ([source](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/2190286))

## Churches, Towers, Monuments
- The old fortified village of Diklo is dated to the late medieval period and listed as a monument of national significance, register entry 7514. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6068808f2b2f2.pdf#page=266))
- Late medieval fortified house of national significance. The Heritage Agency register records it under entry 942, dated 3 October 2007. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6068808f2b2f2.pdf#page=30))
- The ethnographic shrine inventory records Bichekhi, Karate, Sameba and Khakhmati. Individual heritage designation nothing found. ([source](https://www.batsav.com/pages/a-list-of-all-the-shrines-of-pshavi-khevsureti-tusheti.html))
