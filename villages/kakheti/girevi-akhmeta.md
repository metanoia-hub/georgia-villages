## History
- The 1917 Tiflis Governorate table records the settlement as Гиреви and lists all 118 residents as Georgian. ([source](https://pop-stat.mashke.org/georgia-ethnic-loc1917-east.htm))
- Makalatia’s 1933 survey records the following local surnames in 1930–31. Azikuri. The English transcription gives Mozaidze as a tentative reading. ([source](https://www.batsav.com/pages/tusheti-population-census-makalatia.html))
- The village survey names the Azikuri, Khosikuridze and Shuaridze families and notes that people also arrived from neighbouring villages. It records depopulation in the Soviet period. ([source](https://www.dzeglebi.ge/mxareebi_qalaqebi/tusheti/tusheti_girevi.html))
- The 2014 protected-areas management plan dates forced Soviet resettlement from Tusheti to the lowlands to the late 1950s. This describes the regional policy. ([source](https://matsne.gov.ge/ka/document/view/2190286))

## Crafts And Traditions
- Makalatia’s 1930–31 survey records livestock keeping here, with 513 sheep and goats and 100 cattle. ([source](https://www.batsav.com/pages/tusheti-population-census-makalatia.html))
- Village oral history recorded in the survey attributes tower construction to a Kist father and son from Baulo. ([source](https://www.dzeglebi.ge/mxareebi_qalaqebi/tusheti/tusheti_girevi.html))

## Churches, Towers, Monuments
- The historic site of Girevi is dated to the late medieval period and listed as a monument of national significance, register entry 7512. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6068808f2b2f2.pdf#page=266))
- Late medieval tower of national significance. The Heritage Agency register records it under entry 940, dated 3 October 2007. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6068808f2b2f2.pdf#page=30))
- The ethnographic shrine inventory records Holy Mary, Kopale and the Azikuri family shrine. Individual heritage designation nothing found. ([source](https://www.batsav.com/pages/a-list-of-all-the-shrines-of-pshavi-khevsureti-tusheti.html))
