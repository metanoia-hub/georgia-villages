## History
- Tqemakaravi (tqemakaravi, Ткемакарави) is documented by 1835–36 in the Ottoman census of the Çürüksu district. The municipal plan identifies the Istanbul archive register as NFS.d 02763. ([source](https://kobuleti.gov.ge/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/sivrcitimowyoba.pdf#page=109))
- A study citing Zakaria Chichinadze’s village-by-village record of muhajir emigration reports that 10 Tqemakaravi households left for Ottoman territory in the aftermath of the Russo-Ottoman War of 1877–78. ([source](https://nbi.openjournals.ge/index.php/nbi/article/download/10374/10006/17806#page=5))
- The 1923 household roll names families including Kenchishvili (kenchishvili), Tkhilaishvili (tkhilaishvili), Motsqobili (motsqobili), Tsetskhladze (tsetskhladze), Okropiridze (okropiridze) and Makharadze (makharadze). ([source](https://journal.archives-ajara.gov.ge/index.php/home/article/download/108/87/235#page=12))
- In December 2018 the Economy Ministry’s planned solar-panel distribution list named eight families in Tqemakaravi. The programme covered villages with permanent or seasonal use. ([source](https://mtisambebi.ge/news/people/item/854-ushuqod-darchenil-romel-soplebs-gadaszems-mtavroba-mzis-panelebs))
- Founding date, confirmed cause of the modern population decline, and the last permanent household or departure year — nothing found.

## Population Over Time (Other Sources)
- 1835–36 — 102 households and 428 males in the Ottoman census, as transcribed in Kobuleti’s municipal plan. ([source](https://kobuleti.gov.ge/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/sivrcitimowyoba.pdf#page=109))
- 1879 — 43 households in Aleksandr Frenkel’s published survey, as extracted in the municipal plan. ([source](https://kobuleti.gov.ge/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/sivrcitimowyoba.pdf#page=112))
- 1879 — 50 households and 211 males in Petre Umikashvili’s manuscript dated 20 December, as transcribed in the municipal plan. ([source](https://kobuleti.gov.ge/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/sivrcitimowyoba.pdf#page=113))
- 1887 — 166 residents (87 male and 79 female) in 38 households, in the military-statistical survey by V. Lisovsky as transcribed in the municipal plan. The year is the survey’s publication year. ([source](https://kobuleti.gov.ge/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/sivrcitimowyoba.pdf#page=114))
- 1923 — 389 residents (195 male and 194 female) across 62 numbered households in the Adjara State Archive agricultural census roll, fund R-178, inventory 1, case 29, folios 94–95. ([source](https://journal.archives-ajara.gov.ge/index.php/home/article/download/108/87/235#page=13))
- 1923 — 388 people, all recorded as Georgian, in the digitized transcription of the central statistical office’s published settlement totals. The archive household roll gives 389. ([source](https://pop-stat.mashke.org/georgia-ethnic-loc1923.htm))
- 1989 — 6 residents in the Soviet census, as reported by Kobuleti’s municipal plan for the Chakhati rural council. ([source](https://kobuleti.gov.ge/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/sivrcitimowyoba.pdf#page=123))
- 2002 — 0 permanent residents. Georgia’s census settlement tables, volume II. ([source](https://geostat.ge/media/44560/II-tomi-.pdf#page=32))
- 2014 — 2 permanent residents. The exact total is from a reproduced 2014 census table. ([source](https://www.scribd.com/document/601764604/mosaxleobisricxovnobaadministraciul-teritoriulierTeulebisdasqesismixedviT))
- 2018 — 8 families on the Economy Ministry’s planned solar-panel distribution list. The programme included permanent and seasonal households. ([source](https://mtisambebi.ge/news/people/item/854-ushuqod-darchenil-romel-soplebs-gadaszems-mtavroba-mzis-panelebs))

## Crafts And Traditions

## Churches, Towers, Monuments
- Tqemakaravi arched bridge, a 19th-century bridge over the Kintrishi in the village. The National Agency for Cultural Heritage Preservation lists it as monument 3444, entered on 3 October 2007. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6989e0438f7c0.pdf#page=110))
- Varjanauli Bridge, a late medieval bridge about four kilometres east of Tqemakaravi in the Kintrishi Valley. The agency lists it as monument 3443, entered on 3 October 2007. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6989e0438f7c0.pdf#page=110))
