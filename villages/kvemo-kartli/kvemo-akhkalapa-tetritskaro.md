## History
- The ninth- to tenth-century Likureti Church of the Virgin stands within the former village. A local heritage account says the settlement was called Akhkalapa by Pontic Greek speakers who settled there in the nineteenth century; it says those Greek residents left in the 1920s–1930s, after which ethnic Ossetians settled there and left in the late 1980s. The account does not identify the last individuals or give a reason for the Ossetian departure. ([source](https://georgiantravelguide.com/en/liquretis-ghvtismshoblis-eklesia))
- The 1960 administrative directory lists Kvemo Akhkalapa in Tetritskaro District. A 1976 Russian-language newspaper scan states it then had 27 households; this is evidence it remained inhabited in the Soviet period, not a count of residents. ([source](https://djvu.online/file/KpnepFgpi27bv))
- No first-recorded date earlier than the nineteenth-century account, specific founding event, or named last inhabitants were found in the searched English-, Georgian-, and Russian-language material. Search trail: English; Georgian; Russian. ([source](https://www.google.com/search?q=%22Kvemo+Akhkalapa%22+Tetritskaro+history))
- A Georgian-language article on settlement in Kvemo Kartli says Ossetian residents settled in Kvemo Akhkalapa, among other villages. It does not date the move or explain when or why the village emptied. ([source](https://www.dzeglebi.ge/statiebi/istoria/chamosaxlebebi_qvemoqartlshi.html))
