## History
- No foundation date or first historical mention for the village was found. Atabe is a Khevsureti village beside the Khevsureti Aragvi, at about 1,700 metres. A national tourism route describes it as one of the nearly empty villages along the Gudani–Ukankhadu road; it does not say that the village is abandoned. ([source](https://georgia.travel/gudani-ukankhadu-tanie-lake))
- The 1926, 2002 and 2014 counts were 47, 3 and 3. The sources located do not explain a precise depopulation history or name the current permanent residents. The user’s description of all six villages as having zero or one resident is not supported by the latest named Atabe count I could verify: the 2014 figure is three. ([source](https://explore-georgia.ge/ru/mckheta-mtianeti/dushetskii-municipalitet/atabe))

## Population Over Time (Other Sources)
- 1926: 47 (27 men, 20 women); 2002: 3 (2 men, 1 woman); 2014: 3 (1 man, 2 women). The latest named-village figure located is 2014. The 2024 census release found for this research did not yield a verifiable named-village figure, so I cannot confirm a current count of zero or one. ([source](https://explore-georgia.ge/ru/mckheta-mtianeti/dushetskii-municipalitet/atabe))
- The 2002 census table records 3 residents and identifies them as Georgian. ([source](https://www.geostat.ge/media/44560/II-tomi-.pdf))

## Crafts And Traditions
- A field-recording catalogue documents a Khevsur soul-priest’s invocation at a funeral banquet in Atabe in July 2001. Ethnographic and shrine references identify the village’s Kvirae/Kviria Cross; the sources reviewed do not name a dated annual Atabe feast or a village-specific trade. ([source](https://mapageweb.umontreal.ca/tuitekj/caucasus/soundfiles.htm))
- A research article on Khevsur shrine invocations describes a priestly service at Atabe’s Kvirae shrine and the transmission of the shrine role within a local family. It does not supply an annual feast date. ([source](https://www.academia.edu/1651541/Xevsur_shrine_invocations_iconicity_intertextuality_and_agonism))

## Churches, Towers, Monuments
- The national immovable-monuments list records P. Gogochuri’s dwelling house, dated to the second half of the 19th century, as a municipal monument in Atabe (entry 2352). A regional management plan also lists the late-medieval Kvira Cross shrine. I found no separate national heritage-register status for the shrine. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/5981b446431d1.pdf))
- A regional sacred-sites reference identifies the Kvirae Cross in Atabe and explains its role among Khevsur shrines. ([source](https://experience.khconsult.cz/?p=231))
