## History
- Sijanani lies on the right bank of the Bakurkhevi River in Gudamaqari community. An ethnographic study describes village neighborhoods associated with the Meqobaurebi, Totiaurebi, and Sijanani groups; it says Sijanani included Tsiklauri and Chokheli families. In the 1926 census Sijanani and Totiaurtkari were enumerated together: 25 households and 149 people (67 men and 82 women). This joint count should not be read as Sijananis population alone. Sources located do not establish the villages founding, why it declined, or its last inhabitants. ([source](https://iverieli.nplg.gov.ge/bitstream/1234/446924/1/Saaragvo_Da_Aragvelebi_Wigni_II.pdf))
- A traditional-religion ethnography identifies Sijanani as a settlement of Wiklauri households and records the local clan and shrine context; it does not document a village feast date or the settlements abandonment. ([source](https://dspace.nplg.gov.ge/bitstream/1234/323332/1/MtiuletisGudamayrisXandosDaChartlosEtnografiuliMasalebi.pdf))

## Churches, Towers, Monuments
- The heritage inventory lists the late-medieval Kviratskhoveli Church and bell tower in Sijanani (inventory date 3 October 2007). The inventorys category/status field is blank, so a protected-category designation is not established by this record. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/5fedb06c6228a.pdf))
