## History
- Baboti ( / Баботи) appears in the 1923 agricultural census under the Gora community. All 362 residents were recorded as Georgian, according to the settlement-level transcription of the 1926 publication. ([source](https://pop-stat.mashke.org/georgia-ethnic-loc1923.htm))
- The 1977 Georgian Soviet Encyclopedia records Baboti in the Pereta village soviet and lists a primary school in Baboti, with the secondary school in Pereta (vol. 2, printed p. 124). ([source](https://georgianencyclopedia.ge/pdf/2.pdf#page=131))
- The 2002 census counted 18 permanent residents. ([source](https://www.geostat.ge/media/44560/II-tomi-.pdf#page=69))
- By 2014 Geostat counted no permanent residents in Baboti. ([source](https://www.geostat.ge/media/20680/2014-wlis-aRweris-ZiriTadi-Sedegebi.pdf#page=81))
- Etaloni reported in October 2018 that Pereta Public School pupils had hiked to Baboti and its surroundings, describing Baboti as an abandoned settlement. ([source](https://www.etaloni.ge/geo/main/index/15547))

## Population Over Time (Other Sources)
- 1923 — 362 residents, all recorded as Georgian. All-Georgian Agricultural Census, settlement results published in 1926, as transcribed in Population Statistics of Eastern Europe. ([source](https://pop-stat.mashke.org/georgia-ethnic-loc1923.htm))
- Circa 1930 — 366 residents in 74 households, all recorded as Georgian. Kapanadze and Mzhavanadze publish this Gora village soviet figure under the spelling Boboti (boboti) in their account of Vani district’s formation in 1930. ([source](https://dspace.nplg.gov.ge/bitstream/1234/345570/1/Matiane_2020_N19.pdf))
- 2002 — 18 permanent residents, 12 men and 6 women. The census lists Georgian as the predominant nationality. Georgia’s State Department of Statistics, census results, vol. II, p. 69. ([source](https://www.geostat.ge/media/44560/II-tomi-.pdf#page=69))
- 2014 — 0 permanent residents. Geostat census, Vani municipality village table. ([source](https://www.geostat.ge/media/20680/2014-wlis-aRweris-ZiriTadi-Sedegebi.pdf#page=81))
- 2024 — 0 residents. Geostat census, as of 14 November 2024, workbook row 808. ([source](https://geostat.ge/media/81091/1.Population-of-Georgia-by-administrative-territorial-units-and-sex.xlsx))

## Crafts And Traditions
- The Georgian Soviet Encyclopedia’s 1977 entry associates Baboti with the Pereta collective farm, whose listed activities were viticulture, tea cultivation and maize growing (vol. 2, printed p. 124). ([source](https://georgianencyclopedia.ge/pdf/2.pdf#page=131))

## Churches, Towers, Monuments
- The medieval church ruins at Baboti’s old cemetery are a registered architectural monument, number 669. The agency records designation on 30 March 2006 under order 3/133. ([source](https://memkvidreoba.gov.ge/api/Immovable/GetImmovableObject?id=10258))
- The agency’s published register records the Baboti cemetery site as entered on 3 October 2007. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6068808f2b2f2.pdf#page=22))
- Baboti’s Tamar church ruins are a registered architectural and archaeological monument, number 7844. The agency locates them at Pereta in the Chvaneti valley, Vani municipality, and records designation on 12 October 2020 under order 02/69. ([source](https://memkvidreoba.gov.ge/api/Immovable/GetImmovableObject?id=39248))
- The published register records entry on 2 November 2020 and dates the Tamar ruins to the Middle Ages, with an early medieval date proposed tentatively. ([source](https://www.heritagesites.ge/uploads/files/6068808f2b2f2.pdf#page=284))
